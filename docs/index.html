Features

Not a random number with a picture on it

Every throw is a physics simulation running in 3D — the dice tumble, hit the walls, bounce off each other and settle wherever they land. The number you get is the number facing up.

Feels like real dice

Gravity, friction, restitution, wall and floor collisions — a full SceneKit physics body for every die. No two throws land the same way, and each one casts a real contact shadow, so you can tell at a glance how high it is still bouncing.

Looks like a real dice tray

A photographed wooden box frames the table, and every die is finished in a real material instead of flat game-art colour: white and green marble, brushed steel, copper, gold and light wood, with beveled edges and engraved numbers.

Seven kinds of die

Mix any of them in one pool, up to ten dice at a time. The coin reads 0 or 1.

Coind4d6 d8d10d12 d20

Four ways to throw

Tap the table, tap a single die, or shake the phone. Long-press a die to pick it up, aim, and flick it in a direction — or drag it off the table to take it out of the pool.

Know the odds before you throw

A readout docked to the edge of the screen shows the mean, minimum and maximum possible total for your pool, updating the moment you add or remove a die.

Every throw is kept

Throws are saved automatically, on the device. The History tab charts your recent totals and lists each throw's individual dice alongside its own mean, min, max and total. Your last seven totals also stay on screen as you play.

Sounds like real dice

Each die gets a fixed, believable sound budget per throw — one knock on first contact, a few more as it settles — instead of a clatter on every bounce, plus a recorded coin-toss for the coin. Sound, haptics and shake sensitivity are all adjustable.

How it works

Three taps from nothing to a number

Build the pool

Tap the plus to fan out the dice. Each one shows the material it is made of. Pick as many as you like, up to ten, and watch the mean, min and max move as you go.

Throw them

Tap the wood, tap a die, shake the phone, or hold one and flick it. The dice tumble across the box and bounce off the walls until they come to rest.

Read the total

The app waits until every die has genuinely stopped, reads the face pointing up, then counts the total up and flashes it. The previous totals slide along behind it.

About the app

Built in SwiftUI, thrown in SceneKit

Dice Throw is a small, native iPhone app. The dice are procedurally generated 3D meshes with beveled edges and photographed material textures; each result is read from the die's actual resting orientation rather than drawn at random. History is stored on the device with SwiftData. The whole source is on GitHub.

Privacy

Your rolls stay on your phone

  • No account, and no name, email, contacts, location or photos — ever.
  • Your dice pool, your history and your settings are stored only on your device.
  • Since 1.1 the app sends anonymous usage events to Firebase Analytics, using the variant with no access to the advertising identifier — so there is no tracking prompt, no advertising, and nothing shared with advertisers or data brokers.
